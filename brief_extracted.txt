
Aaira Basit: Portfolio Homepage Brief
Creative direction, UX, copy and motion specification
Prepared for Aaira Basit · Version 1.0 · October 2026
How to use this document
This is the master blueprint for the homepage of Aaira’s portfolio. It is written so a UI designer can build it in Figma and a developer can build it in code without guessing. Sections 01 to 07 set the strategy and the visual system. Sections 08 to 10 define the page and every word on it. Sections 11 to 15 cover motion, components and handoff.
Three rules apply throughout:
• Anything in [square brackets] is a placeholder that needs a real fact from Aaira. Nothing in this document invents a client result, a number or a testimonial.
• Copy marked DRAFT is written to be edited. Keep the tone, change the facts.
• Decisions already locked: the hero title is Creative Director & Visual Designer; motion design is not claimed as a skill (AI-assisted reels and visuals sit under AI Creative Production); there is no CV or Resume button; the style is fun, mid-brutalist and corporate.
01 · Project Vision
Positioning
Aaira is not a graphic designer with a portfolio. She is a creative lead who builds whole brands: identity, campaigns, print, social systems and the management that keeps them consistent. The site must make that obvious in five seconds.
Positioning line: Creative Director & Visual Designer who builds brands people remember.
Website goals
1. Reposition. Move the perception from “designer who makes posts” to “creative lead who runs a brand’s visual world.”
2. Prove range. Brand identity, campaigns, print, social, presentations and AI-assisted production should all be visible without a click.
3. Convert. A founder, recruiter or agency reaches the contact section or opens the Portfolio PDF within one session.
Audience
• Founders and marketing heads hiring a creative lead, or a studio-level freelancer.
• Recruiters and hiring managers searching for Creative Director, Creative Lead or Brand Designer roles.
• Agencies and consultancies looking for a collaborator who can own a brand end to end.
User psychology
• First five seconds: confidence. An oversized name, a clear role and one line of promise.
• Next ten seconds: proof. Full-screen work arrives before any explanation.
• Then: trust. Structure, restraint and consistency signal a person who can manage brands, not just decorate them.
• Always: memorability. Small surprises (a sticker, a cursor label, a marquee) make the site feel made by someone with taste and a sense of humour.
Design philosophy
Structured like a corporation, behaving like a studio. Strong grids and clear hierarchy carry the credibility. Brutalist weight and playful details carry the personality.
What success looks like
• Visitors reach the first project within one scroll.
• The Manaaq mini-agency block is opened more often than any other interaction (track it).
• Contact clicks and Portfolio PDF downloads are tracked as the two primary conversions.
02 · Creative Direction
Keywords
• Bold: oversized type, thick lines, no timid gestures.
• Confident: short sentences, no apologies, no “passionate about.”
• Editorial: magazine pacing, one idea per screen, big images.
• Strategic: every section earns its place, and work is framed by what it did for the brand.
• Playful: surprises that reward curiosity, never clutter.
• Corporate: grids, alignment, restraint and professional language.
• Unexpected: one broken rule per screen.
• Clean: generous space, few colours, no decoration for its own sake.
The agency test
A visitor should wonder: “Is this a person or a studio?” If a section feels like a Behance grid or a template, rework it.
Voice
First person, direct, specific. Verbs over adjectives.
• Yes: “I build visual systems that help brands communicate with clarity.”
• No: “I am a passionate designer who loves creating beautiful things.”
Never
Gradients, blobs, neon, glassmorphism, stock icons, skill bars, and any CV or Resume button. The portfolio is the resume.
03 · Design Language
Fun + mid-brutalist + corporate, as a recipe:
• Corporate, about 50%: structure.
• Mid-brutalist, about 30%: attitude.
• Fun, about 20%: surprise.
Corporate: what it contributes
Professional typography, a structured 12-column grid, strong hierarchy, large white space, aligned edges and plain-language copy.
Mid-brutalist: what it contributes
Oversized type, 2px black borders, sharp corners, hard offset shadows, large flat colour blocks, visible grid lines, and the occasional broken-grid layout or unexpected image crop. “Mid” means we borrow the structure and weight of brutalism, not its roughness. Alignment stays intact and readability never suffers.
Fun: what it contributes
Hover animations, a custom cursor with labels, sticky elements, floating labels, a rotating sticker, a marquee and playful transitions. The fun lives in movement and microcopy, not in cartoon graphics, so the site never looks childish.
The rule of one
Each screen gets one loud move (a giant number, a sticker, an off-grid image) and the rest stays calm. If two things shout, one has to leave.
Surface rules
• Radius: 0 on cards, images and buttons. Circles are allowed only for stickers and the cursor.
• Borders: 2px solid #111111 for emphasis, 1px at 12% black for grid lines.
• Shadow: only the hard offset shadow, 6px 6px 0 #111111. No blurred shadows anywhere.
• Dividers: full-bleed 2px lines between major sections.

05 · Colour System
Core palette
• Paper #F7F6F3: main background. A warm off-white.
• Ink #111111: text, borders, hard shadows and dark sections.
• Burnt Orange #C85A1E: the single accent. Used for stickers, key numbers, hover states and highlights.
• Graphite #8E8E8E: captions, inactive states and decorative text.
Supporting neutrals
• White #FFFFFF: card surfaces.
• Concrete #E6E3DC: alternate section backgrounds and tag fills.
• Soft Ink #2A2A2A: hover state on black elements.
• Orange Tint #F3D9C9: sticker fills and highlight backgrounds (optional, same hue as the accent).
• Orange Deep #B84E15: button fills that carry white text, and link hover.
Usage ratio
Roughly 70% Paper, 20% Ink, 8% Concrete and White, 2% Orange. Orange is a spice, never a base.
Accessibility
Approximate contrast ratios against Paper. Verify every pair with a contrast checker before launch.
• Ink on Paper: about 17:1. Passes everywhere.
• Burnt Orange on Paper: about 3.9:1. Use for large text (24px and up) and graphics only, never body copy.
• Graphite on Paper: about 3:1. Decorative or large text only. For small captions use #6B6B6B (about 4.9:1).
• White on Orange Deep: about 5:1. Use this pair for filled buttons.
States
• Button hover: fill changes from Ink to Orange Deep, text stays white.
• Card hover: lifts 8px and the hard shadow appears. Border stays Ink.
• Link hover: underline grows from the left and the text turns Orange Deep.
• Focus ring: 2px Orange Deep, 3px offset, on every interactive element.
• Section rhythm: alternate Paper, Ink and Concrete so the page has a beat while scrolling. Expertise and Contact are the Ink sections.
06 · Typography System
Typefaces
• Primary: Satoshi (free on Fontshare) or Neue Montreal (paid). Use one, not both.
• Secondary: Instrument Serif, italic only, at most one word per heading and never more than once per screen.
• Label font (optional): a mono such as IBM Plex Mono or JetBrains Mono for tiny labels and index numbers. This is a suggested addition that pushes the brutalist feel without changing the palette.
Scale (desktop, then mobile)
• Display XL (name in hero): clamp(72px, 14vw, 220px), line height 0.88, letter spacing -0.05em, weight 800 to 900, uppercase. Mobile: 72px.
• H1 (section titles): 96px, line height 0.95, -0.04em, weight 700. Mobile: 48px.
• H2 (expertise items): 72px, line height 1.0, -0.03em, weight 600. Mobile: 40px.
• H3 (project titles): 56px, line height 1.0, -0.03em, weight 700. Mobile: 36px.
• H4 (card titles): 28px, line height 1.15, -0.01em, weight 600. Mobile: 22px.
• Body large: 22px, line height 1.5, weight 400. Mobile: 18px.
• Body: 16px, line height 1.6, weight 400.
• Label: 12px, +0.08em, uppercase, weight 500. Mobile: 11px.
• Navigation: 15px, +0.01em, weight 500.
• Button: 14px, +0.06em, uppercase, weight 600.
• Index numbers (01, 02, 03): 120 to 200px, transparent fill with a 2px Ink outline.
Rules
• Paragraph line length: 62 characters at most.
• No centred paragraphs. Headings may be centred only in the Contact section.
• Hyphenation off.
• Project titles are always sentence-case or uppercase, never mixed.
• The Instrument Serif italic moment is reserved for one emotional word, for example: Building brands people remember.
07 · Grid and Spacing
Grid
• Desktop, 1440 and up: container max width 1440 (full-bleed imagery allowed), 12 columns, 40px margins, 24px gutters.
• Laptop, 1024 to 1439: 12 columns, 32px margins, 20px gutters.
• Tablet, 768 to 1023: 8 columns, 24px margins, 16px gutters.
• Mobile, under 768: 4 columns, 20px margins, 16px gutters. Design at 390px wide.
Spacing scale
4, 8, 16, 24, 40, 64, 96, 160, 240.
• Section padding, top and bottom: 160px desktop, 96px tablet, 64px mobile.
• Gap between heading and content: 64px desktop, 40px mobile.
Visible grid
Show the 12-column lines at 12% black opacity in the hero and the About section. Pressing the G key toggles a full grid overlay for every visitor. It is a small easter egg that tells design-literate visitors the structure is intentional.

11 · Motion Direction
Motion should feel confident and a little cheeky: quick on hover, slower and weightier on reveals. Nothing bounces on text, and nothing moves without a reason.
Tokens
• Ease out (default entrance and hover): cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)
• Ease in-out (curtains, panels): cubic-bezier(0.76, 0, 0.24, 1)
• Ease back (stickers, buttons popping in): cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1)
• Durations: fast 200ms, base 250ms, medium 500ms, slow 700ms, hero 900ms
Hover specifications
• Buttons: scale 100% to 102%, 250ms ease-out. Fill Ink to Orange Deep. Arrow icons shift 4px.
• Cards and panels: lift 8px, hard shadow (6px 6px 0 Ink) appears, 250ms ease-out. Border stays Ink.
• Project images: zoom 100% to 104%, 600ms ease-out, on an overflow-hidden frame so the border never moves.
• Navigation links: underline grows from the left, 200ms ease-out, and shrinks to the right on exit.
• Expertise rows: text turns Orange and indents 24px, 250ms.
• Client names: hovered name turns Orange, the rest fall to 25% opacity, 250ms.
• Tags: fill flips from transparent to Ink with Paper text, 200ms.
Scroll behaviour
• Section reveals: opacity 0 to 100% and translateY 40px to 0, 700ms ease-out, 80ms stagger between siblings. Trigger when 15% of the element is in view. Play once only.
• Heading reveals: each line sits in an overflow-hidden mask and rises from 100% to 0, 900ms, 100ms stagger between lines.
• Parallax: project images move -8% to +8% of their height across the viewport. The About portrait uses a factor of 0.9. Never parallax text.
• Marquee: 40s per loop, linear. Scroll velocity adds up to 1.5x speed.
• Smooth scrolling (desktop only): a Lenis-style lerp with a duration around 1.1s. Keep native scrolling on touch devices.
• Sticky elements: navigation, Manaaq title, Experience year numeral. Each sticky element must release cleanly at the end of its section.
Custom cursor (desktop with a mouse only)
• Default: a 12px dot, Ink on Paper sections and Paper on Ink sections, following the pointer with a lerp of about 0.2.
• Over links and buttons: grows to a 48px outlined circle, 200ms.
• Over projects: grows to a 96px Orange circle with an Ink label (VIEW), 300ms.
• Over Manaaq panels: same circle with the label OPEN ↘.
• Over the email link: label COPY.
• Never hide the system cursor on form fields. Disable the custom cursor entirely on touch devices and under reduced motion.
Page and state transitions
• Project pages open with an Ink curtain, 600ms ease in-out, with the project name set large during the cover.
• Accordions (Manaaq, Experience, Expertise on mobile) animate height over 400 to 500ms and never jump.
Reduced motion
Under prefers-reduced-motion: no parallax, no smooth scroll, no custom cursor, no marquee movement, a static sticker, and every reveal becomes a 200ms opacity fade. The site must read as complete with zero motion.
Motion don’ts
• No animation over one second except the loader and the hero sequence.
• No bouncing or wobbling text.
• No autoplaying sound.
• No looping animation other than the marquee and the sticker.
• No more than one element animating for attention in the same viewport.
12 · Brutalist Elements
Use these where the structure can take them, and never all at once.
• Huge outlined numbers: project indexes (01, 02, 03) and the experience years. Transparent fill, 2px Ink outline.
• Outlined buttons: secondary actions use a 2px Ink border, no fill, and fill with Ink on hover.
• Oversized labels: section labels (SELECTED WORK, ABOUT) at Label size but with wide tracking, set against a huge heading.
• Unexpected image crops: project images are cropped off-centre, with a subject cut by the edge, at least once per project.
• Rotated stickers: three on the whole page (hero, About, Contact), each 96 to 160px, tilted between -12° and 12°.
• Hard shadows and thick borders: on info panels, tags and floating cards.
• Full-bleed dividers: 2px Ink lines between major sections.
• Grid overlays: the 12% grid lines in the hero and About, and the G-key overlay.
• Marquee strips: one under the hero, and optionally one above the footer.
Limits
• One rotated element per screen at most.
• If a screen already has a giant outlined number, skip the sticker on that screen.
• Body copy always sits on a flat, plain surface. Brutalism lives around the text, never behind it.

14 · Copywriting Principles
The homepage copy in Sections 09 and 10 follows these rules. Use them for every new line added later.
• Lead with the outcome, not the title. Instead of “I am a designer who…”, write “Building brands people remember.”
• Verbs over adjectives. Build, direct, launch, shape, run.
• Short sentences. If a line needs a comma to breathe, it is probably two lines.
• Specific beats impressive. One real proof point beats three adjectives.
• First person, plain words.
• Avoid: passionate, detail-oriented, hard-working, creative thinker, unique, innovative, rockstar, ninja, guru.
• Use: build, direct, systems, campaigns, clarity, consistency, memorable, launch.
• Never claim what the work cannot show. Motion design is not claimed. AI-assisted production is described as exactly that.
Before and after
• Before: “I am a passionate designer with experience in social media.” After: “I build social systems that stay consistent at volume.”
• Before: “Detail-oriented Visual Communications Designer.” After: “Creative Director & Visual Designer.”
• Before: “Worked on several brands.” After: “One parent company, three brands, one creative lead.”
